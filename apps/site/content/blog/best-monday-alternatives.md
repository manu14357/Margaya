monday.com is a construction kit. Boards, column types, automations and dashboards can be put together into a CRM, a content calendar, a hiring pipeline or a project tracker, and business teams can do it without asking anyone for help. That flexibility is the reason people choose it.

It's also why replacing it is tricky. Two teams that both "use monday" may have built completely different things. So this guide doesn't rank tools in one list. It starts from what your boards actually do, then points to the tools that do that job best.

We build [Margaya](/monday-alternative), which is one of the options. We've described it as plainly as the others, including where it isn't the right choice.

## Start from what your boards do

Open your workspace and look at the five boards people use most. Most fall into one of these groups:

| Your boards are mostly... | Look first at |
| --- | --- |
| Projects with tasks, owners and deadlines | Margaya, Asana, Wrike |
| Pipelines for non-project work (sales, hiring, content) | ClickUp, Notion, Trello |
| Docs and wikis with some tracking attached | Notion, ClickUp |
| Software work with sprints and bugs | Margaya, Linear |
| Something that must run on your own servers | Margaya, OpenProject, Vikunja |

If your boards are mostly CRM-style pipelines with heavy automation, a project tool won't replace them well. Look at tools built for that, and keep project work separate.

## The options in detail

### Margaya

We build Margaya, so read this section with that in mind.

**Good fit if** your boards are really projects: tasks with owners, statuses and dates. Every project has a board, a list, a backlog, a spreadsheet, a calendar and a Gantt timeline over the same tasks, so people can work in the layout they prefer without duplicating anything. Custom fields, labels, sub-tasks and task relations cover most of what monday's columns do for project work.

Heavier planning tools are switched on per project only when a team needs them: cycles for sprints, modules for larger pieces of work, estimates, time tracking, and intake for requests from outside the team. Built-in automations archive finished tasks and close stale ones after a preview, and webhooks plus a REST API handle the rest. Pages keep specs and notes next to the work. It's MIT licensed, and you can run it on your own server with every feature or use Margaya Cloud, hosted in the EU.

**Watch out for:** Margaya isn't a general "work OS". It has no CRM-style boards, no form builder beyond intake requests and public boards, no formula columns, and its built-in automations are deliberately few. If your monday workspace is mostly pipelines and automations, it won't replace that.

### Asana

**Good fit if** you coordinate projects across departments and want goals, portfolios and workload above them. Asana's rules, forms and timelines are mature, and non-technical teams pick it up quickly.

**Watch out for:** it's hosted only, and the features that set it apart sit on the higher plans.

### ClickUp

**Good fit if** you want one place for nearly everything: tasks, docs, whiteboards, goals, chat and many views. Its flexibility is closest to monday's.

**Watch out for:** that flexibility needs someone to set conventions, or the workspace gets busy. Decide early which parts you'll use.

### Trello

**Good fit if** your boards are simple and your team is small. Lists and cards are instantly clear, and Power-Ups and built-in automation fill in the gaps.

**Watch out for:** it's narrower than monday. Cross-board reporting and timelines lean on add-ons and higher plans.

### Notion

**Good fit if** your team works from documents. Its databases can become a tracker inside your wiki, with relations, views and templates.

**Watch out for:** you build and maintain the system yourself, and task-specific features like notifications and dependencies are thinner than in a dedicated tool.

### Wrike

**Good fit if** you're a larger organisation that needs Gantt charts, workload and resource planning, request forms, approvals and proofing.

**Watch out for:** it's a big tool with a learning curve, and more than most small teams need.

### Basecamp

**Good fit if** you want less structure, not more: message boards, to-dos, schedules and docs, with almost nothing to configure. It's strong for client work.

**Watch out for:** it deliberately leaves out custom fields, sprints and detailed dependencies.

### OpenProject

**Good fit if** you must host it yourself and want structured planning: work package hierarchies, Gantt charts, time and costs, meetings and a wiki.

**Watch out for:** a heavier interface than monday's, and some capabilities kept for the paid Enterprise edition.

### Vikunja

**Good fit if** you want a small, open-source task manager on your own server, with list, Kanban, Gantt and table views.

**Watch out for:** it's aimed at individuals and small teams. Collaboration and planning features are lighter than in the other tools here.

## How to choose in an afternoon

1. **List your top five boards** and what each one is for.
2. **Mark the must-haves.** Usually two or three things: a timeline, a form, a particular automation, a client-facing view.
3. **Cut the list to two tools** using the tables above.
4. **Rebuild one real board in each** and use it for a week. The one people return to without being asked is the right one.

## Moving off monday.com

monday.com can export a board to Excel. Save it as CSV and most tools on this list can read it.

In Margaya, open **Settings** > **Projects** > your project > **General**, choose **Import**, and pick the CSV file. You map each column to a Margaya field, such as title, description, status, priority, assignees, labels and dates. A review step shows what will happen before anything is created. Statuses are matched to the project's columns by name, and anything that doesn't match lands in the backlog rather than failing. People are matched to workspace members by email or exact name.

Before you import:

- **Flatten groups.** monday's groups don't map to one field everywhere. Turn them into a column, such as a label, before you export.
- **Invite the team first,** so assignees match.
- **Rebuild automations by hand.** No tool imports another tool's automations. List the ones you rely on and recreate the few that still matter.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a free alternative to monday.com?

Open-source tools such as Margaya, OpenProject's community edition and Vikunja are free to run on your own server. You pay for hosting and the time to maintain it. Most hosted tools here also offer free plans with limits.

### Can monday.com be self-hosted?

No, it's a hosted service. Margaya, OpenProject and Vikunja can all run on your own infrastructure.

### Which alternative is most like monday.com?

ClickUp is the closest in flexibility, and Asana and Wrike in project and portfolio features. Margaya is closer to a focused project tracker than to a general work platform.

### Will my automations come across?

No tool imports another tool's automations. Treat the move as a chance to keep only the ones that still earn their place.

## The short version

If your monday.com workspace is a set of business pipelines with heavy automation, look at ClickUp or Notion, or keep monday for that work. If it's mostly projects, Asana and Wrike are the closest hosted options, and Trello or Basecamp are lighter. If you want an open-source project tool you can host yourself, with every layout over the same tasks and optional sprints, docs and intake, try Margaya. Either way, rebuild one real board before you move everything.
