title: Account Security
description: Reset a forgotten password, change it, change or verify your email, review where you're signed in and leave a workspace

Password, email verification and your signed-in devices live under **Settings -> Account -> Security**.

## Forgot your password

When your server can send email and password sign-in is on, the sign-in screen shows **Forgot password?** next to the password field.

1. Select **Forgot password?** and enter your account email.
2. Open the email titled **Reset your Margaya password** and select **Reset password**.
3. Enter a new password twice and select **Set new password**.
4. Sign in with the new password.

- The confirmation looks the same whether or not an account uses that email, so the form never reveals who has an account.
- A reset link works once and expires after **1 hour**. An old or used link shows **This reset link is invalid or has expired**; request a new one from there.
- Setting a new password signs out **every** session of your account, on every device.
- New passwords follow the [password rules](#password-rules).

<Note>
  Without email configured on the server, the link is not shown. Ask an instance admin to reset access for you.
</Note>

## Change your password

The **Password** section appears when your account signs in with a password (accounts that only use GitHub, Google, Discord or single sign-on don't have one).

1. Enter your **Current password**, then the **New password** twice.
2. Keep **Sign out other sessions** checked (the default) to sign out every other device. Your current device stays signed in.
3. Select **Change password**.

<Screenshot src="account/password" alt="The Password section with current password, new password and confirmation fields, the Sign out other sessions option and the Change password button" caption="Change your password under Settings > Account > Security." />

A wrong current password is refused and nothing changes.

## Password rules

Every new password (at sign-up, when you reset or change it) must:

- have at least **10 characters**;
- not be a common password such as `1234567890`, `qwertyuiop` or a common word with numbers added (`Password2024!`);
- not be your email address.

A password you already have keeps working until you change it. A few unrelated words make a strong password that is easy to remember.

## Change your email address

You need email configured on the server. Guest accounts can't change their address.

1. Go to **Settings -> Account -> Information** and select **Change email** under your email address.
2. Enter the new address and select **Send code**.
3. Enter the 6-digit code sent to the **new** address (it expires after 5 minutes) and select **Change email**.

Your email changes only once the code is entered. Afterwards:

- your old address gets an email saying the address changed;
- every other session of your account is signed out (this device stays signed in);
- you sign in with the new address from now on.

If you signed in more than a day ago, sign in again first.

## Verify your email

Workspace invitations sent to your address only show in Margaya once the address is verified, so that someone who registers with your email can't see them. Accounts created with a password start unverified; accounts from GitHub, Google and most single sign-on providers, or from an email code, are usually verified already.

1. Open **Invitations** in the Home panel, or **Settings -> Account -> Security**.
2. Under **Verify your email**, select **Send code**.
3. Enter the 6-digit code from the email (it expires after 5 minutes) and select **Verify**.

Your pending invitations appear right away. The emailed invitation link works whether or not your address is verified. Verification needs email to be configured on the server.

## Active sessions

**Active sessions** lists every device signed in to your account, newest activity first, with:

- the browser and operating system, read from the browser's user agent;
- when the session started and when it was last active (refreshed about once a day of use, so this is approximate);
- the IP address it signed in from, when known;
- a **This device** badge on the session you are using.

<Screenshot src="account/sessions" alt="The Active sessions list with each signed-in browser, when it was last active and signed in, its IP address, and Sign out buttons" caption="Every device signed in to your account." />

Select **Sign out** on a session you don't recognize, or **Sign out all other sessions** to keep only this one. Signed-out devices must sign in again. To end the current session, use **Sign out** in the account menu.

## Two-factor authentication

If you sign in with a password, you can ask for a second step: a 6-digit code from an authenticator app (any app that supports TOTP, such as 1Password, Bitwarden, Google Authenticator or Authy).

1. Go to **Settings -> Account -> Security** and select **Turn on** under **Two-factor authentication**.
2. Confirm your password.
3. Add the **setup key** to your authenticator app (or select **Open in an authenticator app on this device**), then type the 6-digit code it shows and select **Verify**.
<Screenshot src="account/two-factor-setup" alt="The Two-factor authentication section during setup, with the setup key, a link to open it in an authenticator app and the field for the 6-digit code" caption="Add Margaya to your authenticator app, then enter its code." />

4. Save the 10 **recovery codes** somewhere safe. Each one signs you in once if you lose your device.

From then on, password sign-in asks for a code. Select **Trust this device for 30 days** to skip it on that browser for a while, or **Use a recovery code** if you don't have your device. **New recovery codes** replaces the old ones, and **Turn off** removes two-factor; both ask for your password.

<Note>
While two-factor is on, Margaya doesn't send you sign-in links or sign-in codes by email, because they would skip the second step. On a server that signs in with email codes, select **Sign in with a password instead**. Signing in with GitHub, Google, Discord or single sign-on uses that provider's own checks.
</Note>

## API keys

Create keys under **Settings -> Account -> API keys** with **Create API key**, to use the REST API, the CLI or MCP. Give the key a name and an **Expiration** (1, 7, 30 or 90 days, a custom date or **Never**), then choose its **Access**:

- **Full access**: everything you can do, in every workspace you belong to. Keys made before scoping work this way.
- **Read-only**: it can read, and every request that would change something is refused (403).
- **Custom**: pick **Read** or **Write** for each area (projects, tasks, labels, cycles, modules, views, intake, pages, analytics, workspace, audit log). Areas you leave at **None** are refused.

<Screenshot src="account/api-key-create" alt="The Create API key dialog with a name, the expiration, Custom access with Read or Write per area, and the Limit to specific projects option" caption="A custom key gets only the areas you choose." />

Select **Limit to specific projects** to pick projects from the current workspace. Other projects then look missing to the key (404), and workspace-wide requests such as search are refused; the project list shows only its projects. A key never does more than your own role allows, and limited keys can't manage your account or other keys.

The key itself is shown once, right after you create it. The **Scope** column shows each key's access, next to when it expires and was last used.

<Screenshot src="account/api-keys" alt="The API keys list with a read-only key Weekly report and a custom key CI pipeline limited to Website relaunch" caption="Your API keys and what each one may do." />

The activity icon on a key opens its **request log**: the time, method, route (such as `/api/task/:id`), status and duration of recent requests. Request contents are never stored. Entries are kept 14 days by default.

<Screenshot src="account/api-key-requests" alt="The request log of the Weekly report key with the time, method and route, status and duration of each request" caption="The request log shows what a key did, never the request contents." />

## Leave a workspace

1. Switch to the workspace and go to **Settings -> Workspace -> General**.
2. Under **Danger zone**, select **Leave workspace** and confirm.

You lose access to its projects, tasks and pages at once; someone has to invite you again for you to come back. Margaya then opens another of your workspaces, or workspace setup if you have none left.

The **only owner** of a workspace can't leave it. Transfer ownership to another member first (**Transfer ownership** on the same page), or delete the workspace.

## Sign-in limits

To slow down password guessing, the server limits how often one address can try to sign in, request codes or reset a password. When you hit a limit you'll see **Too many requests**; wait a minute and try again. See [Environment variables](/core/installation/environment-variables#sign-in-rate-limiting) for self-hosting details.
