title: Collaborate with Your Team
description: Invite members, assign ownership, and keep team execution aligned
---

Good collaboration in Margaya comes from clear ownership and consistent project conventions.

## 1. Invite members

In your workspace:

1. Open **Settings** > **Members**.
2. Click **Invite member**. The **Invite team member** dialog opens.
3. Enter one or more email addresses, separated by commas, spaces or new lines.
4. Pick a role: viewer, member, admin, [guest](/core/functional/guest-access) or one of your workspace's custom roles. You can only give a role up to your own: a role that grants any permission yours doesn't is not offered, and the server refuses it.
5. Click **Send invitation**. They go out one at a time; any that fail stay in the box with the reason, so you can fix them and send again.

<Screenshot src="tasks/invite-member" alt="The Invite team member dialog with two email addresses, the Member role selected and the Send invitation button" caption="Invite several people at once, with one role." />
6. Track active and pending members in the members table. To change what a pending invitation grants, pick a new role on its row.
7. Click a member's name to open their [profile](/core/functional/member-profiles): their workload, assigned tasks and recent activity.

### Remove a member

In the members table, select the remove button on the person's row, then **Remove member**. You can't remove yourself this way, and removing members is for Owners and Admins by default.

Removing someone ends their access to the workspace at once, whichever session or API key they use, because every request checks membership. Their open boards and pages stop updating, and their project memberships in this workspace are deleted, so inviting them again doesn't bring those back.

What happens to their sessions and API keys depends on what's left:

| Situation | Sessions | API keys |
| --- | --- | --- |
| This was their last workspace | Ended everywhere | All deleted |
| They're still in another workspace | Stay signed in | Keep working in their other workspaces |
| They're an instance admin | Stay signed in | Keep working |

A key limited to specific projects loses this workspace's projects, and a key with no project left is deleted. A workspace's admins can't sign someone out of another workspace or break their keys there.

Deleted keys appear in the [audit log](/core/functional/audit-log) as revoked. Ended sessions appear in the instance [audit trail](/core/installation/instance-admin#audit-trail).

The account itself stays. To stop someone using the server at all, an instance admin can [ban the account](/core/installation/instance-admin#users), which signs them out everywhere and stops all their API keys.

## 2. Assign clear ownership

For each active task:

- Assign one primary owner
- Use due dates for time-sensitive work
- Set priority to support tradeoff decisions

## 3. Standardize labels

Use labels to create a shared language across the team. Manage them at the workspace level in **Settings -> Workspace -> Labels** (see [Manage workspace labels](/core/functional/manage-workspace-labels)).

Examples:

- Work type: `bug`, `feature`, `chore`
- Domain: `api`, `web`, `infra`
- Risk/impact: `critical`, `customer-impact`

## 4. Use views intentionally

- **Board** for daily execution
- **List** for compact review and cleanup
- **Backlog** for planning and grooming

Switch views depending on the meeting or decision you are making.

## 5. Keep communication in the task

When scope, ownership, or timing changes, update the task directly so project history remains visible.

### Reply in threads

Answer a comment where it was asked instead of starting a new one: choose **Reply** under the comment, write your answer and choose **Send reply** (or press **Ctrl+Enter**, **Cmd+Enter** on a Mac). **Esc** closes the reply box.

- Replies sit indented under their comment, oldest first. Threads are one level deep: a reply to a reply joins the same thread.
- A thread with more than three replies shows the newest three; **Show N more replies** opens the rest.
- You edit or delete your replies like your comments. Deleting a comment keeps its replies: they stay in the activity as ordinary comments.
- A reply notifies the people already in the thread (whoever wrote the comment and everyone who replied), as well as the task's assignees and subscribers. You are never notified of your own reply, and muting a task silences its threads too.
- Replying under an [AI agent's](/core/functional/ai-agents) comment asks that agent, as an @mention would.
- Viewers can read threads but can't reply, as with comments.
- When the project has a [public board](/core/functional/publish-a-project-board#internal-and-public-comments) with comments on, a reply under a public comment can be **Public** too. Replies start as **Internal**, and replies to internal comments stay internal.
