---
title: Plan Work in Cycles
description: Run time-boxed iterations with automatic status, burndown tracking, and carryover
---

A cycle is a time-boxed stretch of a project's work, like a sprint. Cycle status is derived automatically from its dates, so you never have to update it by hand.

## Turn on Cycles

1. Go to **Settings**.
2. Open **Projects** and select your project.
3. Open the **Features** tab.
4. Turn on **Cycles**.

## Cycle status

Status is computed from today's date, not set by hand. "Today" is the date in the workspace's time zone (**Settings -> Workspace -> General -> Time zone**, UTC unless changed), so a cycle that ends on a given day ends at midnight in that time zone:

- **Draft** - no dates yet
- **Upcoming** - dates set, start date in the future
- **Active** - today falls between the start and end date
- **Completed** - the end date has passed
- **Archived** - manually archived after completion

## Create a cycle

1. Open the project's **Cycles** view.
2. Click **New cycle**.
3. Add a **Name** (e.g. "Sprint 12") and optional **Description**.
4. Set **Dates**, or leave them empty to save it as a draft.
5. Set an **Owner**. It defaults to you.

<Screenshot src="cycles/new-cycle-dialog" alt="The New cycle dialog with the name Sprint 16, a description, Dates set to No dates (draft) and the owner Elena Marsh" caption="Leave the dates empty to keep the cycle as a draft." />

Dated cycles can span at most 366 days, and a cycle's dates can't overlap another dated cycle in the same project, archived ones included. If the dialog reports an overlap, pick different dates or clear them to keep the cycle as a draft.

Once a cycle is active, its start date is locked and its end date can't move into the past.

## Add and remove tasks

- Drag tasks into a cycle from the backlog or board, or use **Add existing tasks** to pull in tasks already in the project.
- A task can only be in one cycle at a time; adding it to a cycle moves it out of any other.
- Tasks can always leave a cycle, even a completed or archived one.

## Check capacity while planning

The cycle planner (**Plan cycle**) shows a capacity check under the cycle's totals, for people who can read analytics: for example **Planned at 132% of a usual cycle**.

- **Capacity** is what the team actually finished in comparable time before. Margaya uses up to 6 ended cycles of the project (at least 2), scaled to this cycle's length. Without enough cycles, it uses the project's daily throughput over the last 8 weeks.
- **Load** is the cycle's tasks that aren't cancelled. When the project's estimates are points or time and most tasks are sized, it compares estimate points; otherwise task counts.
- **Over capacity** means the load is above a usual (median) cycle. **Capacity details** then lists not-started tasks you could move to a later cycle: lowest priority first, then those blocking the fewest other tasks.
- People who can update cycles also see the load per person against that person's own usual pace, to help balance work. It is not a performance score.

<Screenshot src="insight/cycle-capacity" alt="The cycle planner for Sprint 14 warning Over capacity: planned at 130% of a usual cycle, with Capacity details open: 30 points planned against a usual 23, three tasks that could move to a later cycle, and the load per person" caption="Capacity details suggest what could move to a later cycle." />

The check is computed, never written by AI, and it updates as you move tasks in and out.

## Retrospectives

On a running or ended cycle, **Retro** opens a fact sheet of what happened, computed from the cycle's history:

- **Say/do**: how much of the work committed at the start was done.
- **Scope change**: tasks added and removed during the cycle.
- **Carry-over**: tasks moved to another cycle after it ended, and tasks left open.
- **Reopened** tasks, **cycle-time outliers** (tasks that took longer than the project's usual 85th percentile), **blocked** tasks, and how the earliest shared [stakeholder brief](/core/functional/ai-triage-and-briefs) forecast held.

The facts are about the team's work only; there is no per-person scoring. With AI on for the project, **Write narrative** adds a short summary and 3 to 5 discussion questions written from those facts and citing tasks; you can edit it before saving. **Save as page** creates a Page in the project with the facts, the narrative (if any) and empty **What went well**, **What to improve** and **Actions** sections for the team.

## Track progress

The **Cycles** view groups cycles into Active, Upcoming, Draft and Completed. The active cycle opens with its progress, a burndown chart and its high-priority tasks; **Open cycle** shows all of its tasks.

<Screenshot src="cycles/cycle-list" alt="The Cycles view with Sprint 14 active at 27% complete, its progress, burndown and high-priority tasks, and Sprint 15 upcoming" caption="The Cycles view with the active sprint expanded." />

Open a cycle to see its tasks next to its counts, completion percentage, and a burn-down/burn-up chart in tasks or points, broken down by status, assignee, label, and priority. Cancelled tasks are tracked separately and never count as in-progress work.

<Screenshot src="cycles/cycle-detail" alt="Sprint 14 open as a board with its side panel showing 7 days left, 27% progress, the estimate total, a burn-down chart, the dates, owner and a breakdown by status" caption="A cycle shows its tasks next to its progress and burndown." />

## Carry work forward

When a cycle completes, use **Transfer** to move its unfinished tasks (backlog, not started, or started) into a draft, upcoming, or active cycle. Completed and cancelled tasks stay behind, and the source cycle's progress report freezes at the moment of transfer so its historical numbers never change.

To do this automatically, turn on **Roll over unfinished cycle work** under **Settings -> Projects -> [your project] -> Workflow -> Scheduled automations** (see [scheduled automations](/core/functional/automations#roll-over-unfinished-cycle-work)). When a cycle ends in the workspace's time zone, its unfinished tasks move to the next cycle, and the project lead gets a summary.

<Screenshot src="cycles/rollover-setting" alt="The Scheduled automations section of the project's workflow settings with the Roll over unfinished cycle work switch" caption="Roll over is a scheduled automation in the project's workflow settings." />

## Archive a cycle

Archiving is only available for completed cycles. An archived cycle leaves the default list and its favorites are hidden until you restore it with **Unarchive**; nothing about the cycle or its data is deleted.

## Permissions

Working with cycles needs workspace **Cycle** permissions (read, create, update, delete). The built-in Viewer role can only read cycles; Member can create and update them; Admin and Owner can also delete them. Deleting a specific cycle is also allowed for its owner if they have cycle update rights, even without the delete permission. All of this is on top of the Cycles feature being turned on for the project.

## Tips

- Keep cycles short and consistent (one or two weeks) so status and burndown stay meaningful.
- Use **Transfer** instead of manually moving tasks one by one when closing out a cycle.
- A cycle's tasks stay in the project when the cycle is deleted; only the grouping goes away.
