---
title: Trash and Restore
description: Deleted tasks and projects go to the trash first, so you can bring them back before they are deleted permanently
---

Deleting a task or a project does not erase it right away. It moves to the trash, where it stays restorable until someone deletes it permanently or the automatic purge removes it (after 30 days by default).

While something is in the trash, Margaya behaves as if it no longer exists: it disappears from boards, lists, Your work, search, analytics, cycles, modules, the inbox, favorites, recent items, exports, public boards, integrations and the MCP tools. Nothing about it is lost, though: comments, attachments, relations, time entries and history are kept and come back with it.

## Delete a task

Delete a task from its menu, from the task detail, or select several tasks and use **Delete** in the bulk bar. Deleting a task also moves its sub-tasks (at every level) to the trash, together with it.

Task keys are never reused: a new task always gets a new number, even after a deleted task is gone for good.

Requests still waiting in [Intake](/core/functional/intake) are an exception: deleting one removes it permanently.

## Restore a task

1. Go to **Settings** and open **Projects**.
2. Select the project and open **Recently deleted**.
3. Find the task (search by title or key, for example `WEB-12`) and click **Restore**.

Each row shows who deleted the task, when, and when it will be deleted permanently. A task deleted together with its sub-tasks shows how many came along; restoring it brings exactly those back. Sub-tasks you had deleted on their own before stay in the trash until you restore them separately.

<Screenshot src="data/project-trash" alt="The Recently deleted page of Website relaunch with two deleted tasks, one with 2 sub-tasks, and a row menu open on Delete permanently" caption="A project's Recently deleted list, with Restore and Delete permanently on every row." />

A restored task goes back to its column. If that column was deleted in the meantime, the task lands in the project's first not-started column. A task whose project is itself in the trash can't be restored until you restore the project.

## Delete a task permanently

In **Recently deleted**, open a task's **...** menu and choose **Delete permanently**, or click **Empty trash** to delete every task in the project's trash. Both ask for confirmation. Permanent deletes remove the task, the sub-tasks deleted with it, their comments, attachments, relations and time entries, and can't be undone.

## Delete and restore a project

Deleting a project (from its settings or the project list) moves it to the trash with all its tasks. Its board, cycles, modules, pages, views and public board are unavailable while it is there, and its key stays reserved, so no new project can take it.

To bring it back, go to **Settings**, open **Workspace**, then **Recently deleted**, and click **Restore**. The project returns with the tasks that were deleted together with it; tasks deleted on their own earlier stay in the project's own trash. From the same page you can **Delete permanently** a project and everything in it.

## Permissions

- Restoring needs the same permission as deleting: **task:delete** for tasks and **project:delete** for projects. Workspace admins and owners have both by default.
- Deleting permanently needs the same permissions.
- Anyone who can read the project can see its **Recently deleted** list.

## Automatic purge

An hourly job deletes items that have been in the trash for longer than the retention period, in the same way as **Delete permanently**. On self-hosted instances the period is set with `TRASH_RETENTION_DAYS` (default `30`; `0` turns the automatic purge off). See [Environment variables](/core/installation/environment-variables).

## Webhooks, integrations and the API

- `task.deleted` and `project.deleted` mean "moved to the trash", so existing integrations keep working.
- `task.restored` and `project.restored` are sent when something comes back, and `task.purged` and `project.purged` when it is deleted permanently. See [Webhooks](/core/functional/webhooks).
- The API's trash routes list, restore and permanently delete trashed tasks and projects. The MCP `delete_task` tool moves a task to the trash and `restore_task` brings it back.
